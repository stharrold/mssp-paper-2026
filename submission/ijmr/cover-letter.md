Please consider the manuscript "Early Renewal of Accountable Care Organization Agreements to Reset Benchmarks in the Medicare Shared Savings Program: Preregistered Retrospective Cohort Study" for publication in the *Interactive Journal of Medical Research* as an Original
Paper.

In the Medicare Shared Savings Program, benchmarks decide whether an accountable care organization earns shared
savings, and how they are reset shapes which organizations join, stay and save. Earlier studies found that regional benchmarking shifted participation toward providers with low spending
relative to their regions, and that organizations whose benchmarks fell were more likely to leave. The 2019 rules added a third
choice: ending an agreement early to reset the benchmark sooner. Twenty-nine of 334 eligible organizations did so in
2022; to my knowledge, who used this option has not been studied in the peer-reviewed literature.

The study adds three findings. First, early renewers were already low-cost relative to their regions and, in the
BASIC track, more often already bearing two-sided risk, for whom renewal was the only route to the 75% sharing rate
of the ENHANCED track. Second, the simple account that organizations reset to raise their benchmarks does not hold on
average: because early renewers were beating their benchmarks, the own-spending part of a reset offset its regional
gain, and the benchmarks published for 2022, examined post hoc, averaged 1.3% below the year before. Third, spending growth after renewal did not
differ detectably. The results bear on current policy: rules in force since 2024 credit prior
savings, changing this trade-off.

The methods are part of the contribution. The plan, decision rules and code were registered on OSF Registries
(osf.io/c53w9) before any outcome data were read, and the code enforced that order. The counterfactual benchmark, which is not
published, was rebuilt from the regulation and validated before use. The registered estimates are Bayesian, with permutation and simulation tests and a decision model that carries
the rebuilt benchmark's measurement error; every registered conclusion agreed with its conventional frequentist
counterpart, so the conclusions do not depend on the
inferential framework.
