Please consider the manuscript "Early Renewal of Accountable Care Organization Agreements to Reset Benchmarks in the Medicare Shared Savings Program: Preregistered Retrospective Cohort Study" for publication in the Interactive Journal of Medical Research as an Original Paper.

In the Medicare Shared Savings Program, an accountable care organization's benchmark is reset at each new agreement from its own recent spending, adjusted toward its region's. The 2019 rules let organizations end an agreement early to reset sooner, and 29 of 334 eligible organizations did so in 2022. To my knowledge, which organizations used this option has not been studied in the peer-reviewed literature, and it bears on how benchmark rules sort organizations into the program.

The study is a preregistered retrospective cohort study of public Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services files. The analysis plan, decision rules and code were registered on OSF Registries (osf.io/c53w9) before any outcome data were read. The reset benchmark was rebuilt from the regulation and validated against published benchmarks before use. Early renewers' regions spent more relative to their current benchmarks than stayers' regions did, and early renewers were more often at the higher, two-sided risk levels, for which renewal opened a 75% sharing rate. Because they were also beating their benchmarks by more, a reset would not on average have raised their benchmarks, and the benchmarks published for 2022 confirm this. Spending growth afterward did not differ detectably. These results describe selection under benchmark rules that were revised in 2024.
