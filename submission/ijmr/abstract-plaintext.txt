Background: Medicare Shared Savings Program (MSSP) benchmarks are reset at each new agreement from an accountable care organization's (ACO's) recent spending, adjusted toward its region. ACOs could end an agreement early to reset sooner; which ACOs did so has not been studied in the peer-reviewed literature.

Objective: This study aimed to test whether ACOs that renewed early stood to gain more from the regional part of a reset, and whether their spending growth differed afterward.

Methods: This preregistered retrospective cohort study included the 334 MSSP ACOs whose agreements began July 1, 2019 or January 1, 2020, using Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) public use files (2016-2024). Plan, decision rules and code were registered before any outcome data were read. The reset benchmark was rebuilt from 42 CFR 425.601 and validated against CMS's published benchmarks for 101 other ACOs before use. For early renewal into a January 1, 2022 agreement (29 ACOs vs 277 that stayed), the primary measure was the regional pull (case-mix-adjusted regional spending relative to the current benchmark); the own-spending component of a reset and the BASIC risk level were also examined. Groups were compared by Bayesian bootstrap and permutation tests, and early renewal was modeled by Bayesian logistic regression with measurement error, tested by direct simulation. Per-capita spending growth in 2022-2024 was compared by difference-in-differences with a preregistered equivalence condition on pre-trends.

Results: The rebuilt benchmark tracked CMS's (Spearman rho = 0.94). Relative to their current benchmarks, early renewers' regional spending was 3.4 percentage points higher than stayers' (95% credible interval [CrI], 1.7 to 5.4; P<.001), but they were also beating their benchmarks by more (own-spending component, -2.1 points; 95% CrI, -3.8 to -0.5), and the total expected gain did not differ (-0.5 points; 95% CrI, -2.0 to 0.8). Post hoc, CMS's published 2022 benchmarks for early renewers averaged 1.3% below their 2021 benchmarks. Among 206 BASIC-track ACOs, early renewal was associated with a higher regional pull (log-odds difference per 2 SD, 1.34; 95% CrI, 0.44 to 2.30) and a higher, two-sided BASIC level (1.34; 95% CrI, 0.38 to 2.31). Spending growth did not differ detectably (-0.4%; 95% CrI, -4.0% to 2.5%); the equivalence condition did not hold, so this is not read as an effect.

Conclusions: ACOs that renewed early more often had regions that spent more relative to their benchmarks and were more often at higher BASIC levels, for which renewal opened a 75% sharing rate; the reset did not on average raise their benchmarks. These are associations under the 2022 rules; benchmarks set from 2024 credit prior savings.
