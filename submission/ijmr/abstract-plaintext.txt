Background: Medicare Shared Savings Program (MSSP) benchmarks are reset at each new agreement from an accountable care organization's (ACO's) recent spending, adjusted toward its region. ACOs could end an agreement early to reset sooner, which was also the only way for an ACO in the BASIC track to reach the ENHANCED track's 75% share of savings. Why ACOs renewed early has not been studied in peer-reviewed work.

Objective: This study aimed to describe which ACOs renewed early, weighing a reset motive (a higher benchmark) against a sharing-rate motive (the move to ENHANCED), and to compare their spending growth afterward.

Methods: This preregistered retrospective cohort study included the 334 MSSP ACOs whose agreements began July 1, 2019 or January 1, 2020, using Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) public use files (2016-2024). The plan, decision rules and code were registered before outcome data were read. The reset benchmark was rebuilt from 42 CFR 425.601 and validated against published benchmarks for 101 other ACOs before use. For early renewal into a January 1, 2022 agreement (29 ACOs vs 277 that stayed), the primary measure was the regional pull (case-mix-adjusted regional spending relative to the current benchmark); the own-spending component and BASIC risk level were also examined. Groups were compared by Bayesian bootstrap and permutation tests, and early renewal was modeled by Bayesian logistic regression. Spending growth in 2022-2024 was compared by difference-in-differences.

Results: The rebuilt benchmark tracked CMS's (Spearman rho = 0.94). Relative to their current benchmarks, early renewers' regional spending was 3.4 percentage points higher than stayers' (95% credible interval [CrI], 1.7 to 5.4; P<.001), but they were also beating their benchmarks by more (own-spending component, -2.1 points; 95% CrI, -3.8 to -0.5), and the total expected gain did not differ (-0.5 points; 95% CrI, -2.0 to 0.8). Post hoc, CMS's published 2022 benchmarks for early renewers averaged 1.3% below their 2021 benchmarks. Among 206 BASIC-track ACOs, early renewal was associated with a higher regional pull (log-odds difference per 2 SD, 1.34; 95% CrI, 0.44 to 2.30) and a higher BASIC level (levels C-E; 1.34; 95% CrI, 0.38 to 2.31), and 26 of all 27 BASIC-track early renewers moved to ENHANCED. Spending growth did not differ detectably (-0.4%; 95% CrI, -4.0% to 2.5%), and a preregistered pre-trend condition did not hold.

Conclusions: Early renewers were already beating their benchmarks and had more room under their regions' spending; for almost all, renewal was the route to a 75% sharing rate, and the reset did not on average raise their benchmarks. The pattern fits the sharing-rate motive better than the reset motive, though the design cannot separate them. These are associations under rules that changed in 2024.
